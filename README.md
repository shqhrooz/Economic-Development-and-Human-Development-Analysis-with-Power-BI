# Economic Development and Human Development Analysis with Power BI

## Project Overview

This project explores the relationship between economic wealth, economic development, and human development across countries using Microsoft Power BI.

Motivated by an interest in Nordic countries, particularly Norway and Iceland, the project examines a broad range of economic, social, and infrastructure indicators to better understand why national wealth does not always translate into higher living standards.

The analysis compares developed economies such as Switzerland, Norway, and Iceland with countries such as Nigeria and Chad to explore differences in economic performance, human development, infrastructure access, and natural resource management.

## Project Objectives

- Explore the relationship between economic performance and human development.
- Compare countries using economic, social, and infrastructure indicators.
- Investigate whether low inflation necessarily indicates a high level of economic development.
- Examine the relationship between natural resource wealth and public welfare.
- Develop an interactive, data-driven view of international development patterns.

## Data and Indicators

The project incorporates indicators from international data sources, including the World Bank, the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the Energy Institute, OPEC, and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS).

The indicators examined include:

**Economic Indicators**
- Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and GDP per capita
- Gross National Income (GNI) and GNI per capita based on Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
- Exports and imports
- Inflation rate

**Human Development Indicators**
- Human Development Index (HDI)
- Life expectancy
- Mean years of schooling

**Infrastructure and Living Standards**
- Access to electricity
- Internet access
- Access to drinking water
- Access to clean cooking technologies
- Poverty rate

**Natural Resources**
- Oil and natural gas reserves
- Economic rents derived from natural resources

## Methodology

The project involved the following analytical steps:

1. **Data Collection:** Identifying and gathering relevant indicators from reliable international sources.
2. **Data Preparation:** Reviewing indicator definitions, measurement units, and reporting years to improve comparability.
3. **Data Integration:** Organizing the collected datasets for analysis in Power BI.
4. **Data Visualization:** Presenting economic, social, and infrastructure indicators through comparative visualizations.
5. **Comparative Analysis:** Examining differences between selected countries and exploring potential relationships among development indicators.

## Key Analytical Questions

- Does low inflation necessarily indicate a high level of economic development?
- How do GDP per capita and human development indicators differ across countries?
- Does an abundance of natural resources automatically translate into greater public welfare?
- How do infrastructure access and living standards complement traditional measures of economic performance?
- What distinguishes countries that successfully convert economic wealth into broader human development?

## Key Insights

The analysis highlights several important considerations:

- Low inflation, or even negative inflation, is not sufficient on its own to establish that a country is highly developed.
- Economic development cannot be understood through GDP or national income alone; human development and access to essential infrastructure also matter.
- Natural resource abundance does not automatically guarantee higher living standards. Outcomes also depend on how resource wealth is managed and converted into public welfare.
- A multidimensional perspective provides a more comprehensive framework for comparing countries and understanding development outcomes.

These insights reflect the comparisons explored in this project and should be interpreted in the context of the selected countries, indicators, and available data.
